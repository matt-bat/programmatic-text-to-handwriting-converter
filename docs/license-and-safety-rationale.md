# Why this project uses a restrictive license

Scribble Lab is public because people should be able to inspect the code, learn from it, run it locally, and contribute useful improvements. The application is offered without a purchase price for uses allowed by the license.

Public access does not mean the project has no conditions. The source is released under the [PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0](../LICENSE). That choice is intentional.

## Protecting free public access

The project took substantial time to design, test, document, and maintain. The noncommercial license prevents another party from taking the public code and using it in a paid product, profit-seeking hosted service, or other commercial activity without a separate legal basis.

This matters because the project was released for the public to use freely within its permitted scope. It was not released so that an unrelated company could copy the work, remove context, charge for access, and keep the profit.

Optional donations support maintenance. They do not turn the application into a paid service and do not grant commercial-use rights.

## Keeping the official project identity-free

Handwriting software can be misused. A tool that accepts a real person's writing or generates signatures could support impersonation, fraudulent documents, false evidence, or attempts to bypass identity checks.

The official project therefore has a firm technical and contribution boundary. It does not accept handwriting samples, signatures, images, font uploads, author labels, writer matching, tracing, or style extraction. Pull requests and feature requests that add those capabilities will be declined.

The [safety policy](../SAFETY.md) explains this boundary and the uses the project does not support.

## What the license can and cannot do

The PolyForm Noncommercial License primarily limits commercial use. It also includes the controlling warranty and liability terms. The exact license text, not this explanation, determines the legal permissions and conditions.

The license is not a technical lock. Publishing source code means a third party may be able to modify it, including in ways the maintainer rejects. The safety policy and project governance make the official position clear, but they cannot guarantee that no one will ever misuse a public copy.

Fraud, impersonation, forged signatures, and deceptive documents may also violate laws or third-party rights. Those questions depend on the facts and jurisdiction. This document is a statement of project intent, not legal advice.

## Why not use a permissive license

A permissive license would make commercial reuse and resale much easier. That does not match the maintainer's goals for this project. The current license keeps the source available for permitted noncommercial use while reserving commercial exploitation.

Corporate or commercial users who need broader rights can request a separate, more permissive licence from Matthew Bateman through the project's [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/matt-bat/programmatic-text-to-handwriting-converter/discussions). Any such rights require a separate agreement; this contact option does not expand the public licence.

This license choice can reduce adoption by companies and by directories that only list OSI-approved open-source projects. That tradeoff is understood and accepted. Protecting the project's noncommercial purpose and maintaining its safety boundary are more important than maximizing reuse at any cost.
